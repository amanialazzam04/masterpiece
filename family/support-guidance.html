LEARN & SUPPORT

Support & Guidance

Practical ideas for offering care while respecting your loved one's choices and boundaries.

♡

Listen with care

Give them time to speak. Ask open questions, listen without judgment, and let them set the pace.

“Would you like me to listen, or think through options with you?”

✧

Offer practical help

Ask what would make today a little easier. Small, specific offers can be easier to accept than broad promises.

“Would it help if I went with you to look for support?”

○

Respect privacy and choice

Let your loved one decide what to share and with whom. Do not ask for access to their private journal or AI conversations.

“You can share only what feels comfortable.”

☼

Look after yourself

Supporting someone can affect you too. Keep your own support network and make room for rest and boundaries.

You can care deeply and still take time for yourself.

EMOTIONAL WELLBEING

Notice without diagnosing

People respond to stress in different ways. Ask what they have noticed and what support they want; avoid labeling or trying to diagnose them.

Read supportive questions

“What has felt hardest lately?” “What helps you feel a little more supported?” Let them decide how much to share.

COMMUNICATION

Acknowledge their feelings

Reflect what you hear without comparing, dismissing, or immediately trying to fix the feeling.

Read example responses

“That sounds like a lot to manage.” “Thank you for telling me.” “I may not know exactly how it feels, but I want to understand.”

DAILY SUPPORT

Offer specific, small help

Everyday tasks can feel more manageable when support is practical and offered with permission.

Read practical ideas

Ask whether help with a meal, a ride, an errand, or company would be useful. Accept “no” and ask again another time if appropriate.

CAREGIVER WELLBEING

Keep supportive boundaries

Choose realistic ways to help and make time for your own rest, relationships, and support.

Read self-care ideas

Decide when you are available, identify someone you can talk with, and seek your own professional support if the situation is weighing on you.

General educational information only. It does not replace advice from a qualified professional.
